FAQ · THE DETAILS

Good questions.
Straight answers.

What TRAPPUNK can do, what it cannot do, and what you actually need to build one. Select any question to expand the answer.

01Why not just make a mobile phone app?

A mobile app is possible, but it is not automatically a better trainer. Some Android phones have no gyroscope, and sensor performance, screen size and grip vary between devices. A phone held in the hands also feels different from a dedicated training stock. TRAPPUNK uses one known ESP32-S3 board, so we can focus on consistent motion control and touchscreen shooting. A phone version may be explored later.

02Does TRAPPUNK help improve accuracy, reaction time, and stability?

That is exactly what TRAPPUNK is designed to help you practise, and more.

Olympic Trap is a highly standardized competitive shooting discipline governed by strict ISSF rules. Five shooting stations, 15 trap machines, regulated target trajectories, ammunition limits and shooting-range dimensions create a consistent framework for measuring performance. Unlike hunting, Olympic Trap focuses entirely on competitive target shooting. Olympic Skeet is also an ISSF discipline, while Sporting follows a different competitive format.

TRAPPUNK helps you practise reacting to a flying clay at the right moment, moving smoothly, maintaining stability and following through after a virtual shot. You can track your scores, compare sessions with identical CLAY and HIT settings and work toward personal records instead of simply watching spectacular hit animations.

Developing advanced Olympic Trap skills can take years. Although no simulator can guarantee faster progress or replace live shooting, TRAPPUNK offers an affordable way to build consistent training habits before investing in expensive equipment and range time.

03What do I need to build TRAPPUNK, how much does it cost, and can I install it myself?

The supported core is a Waveshare ESP32-S3 Touch LCD 3.49B, hardware V2 (Rev1.1), with its onboard QMI8658 motion sensor. Add a power source and a comfortable cardboard, wooden or other dedicated holder. You can fire virtual shots using the touchscreen. See the BUILD guide.

Allow roughly €20–35 for the board alone, depending on the seller, plus shipping and any holder or power components. Confirm V2 (Rev1.1) before buying; similar-looking revisions are not interchangeable.

You do not need programming experience. On a supported board, the INSTALL page provides a USB browser installer using desktop Chrome or Edge and a USB-C data cable. The installer can detect the ESP32-S3 chip but cannot confirm the exact Waveshare board revision.

Important: first installation or recovery can erase saved sessions and settings. Export anything worth keeping before flashing, and follow the install warnings.

04How realistic are the clay flights and HIT/MISS results?

TRAPPUNK simulates clay trajectories, gun orientation and virtual shot timing using a mathematical model. That can be useful for structured practice, but it is not a certified measurement of real-world hit probability. The clay aerodynamics, shot model and transfer to actual range performance have not been independently validated. Treat virtual scores as simulator scores, not as proof of competition accuracy.

05Do I need Wi-Fi, a PC or an SD card to practise?

No PC, Wi-Fi or cloud account is required for ordinary standalone practice after the firmware is installed. A computer and USB connection are used for browser installation and certain data-analysis workflows. A microSD card is optional for saving or exporting supported training data. The exact available export features depend on the firmware version.

06Why is the first shot so important in Olympic Trap?

A good first-shot hit usually means you recognized the clay's flight early enough and brought your aim onto it without unnecessary correction. The aim is a timely, smooth, controlled movement, not simply pulling the trigger faster.

Second-shot hits still count, but compare your first-shot success, reaction timing and movement across several sessions. A single 25/25 result or a quick virtual shot does not prove good technique. Keep following through after the shot instead of stopping the movement abruptly.

07What do CLAY C1/C2 and HIT H1/H2 actually change?

CLAY C1/C2 changes the target's apparent size on the simulator screen. HIT H1/H2 changes how forgiving the simulated hit zone is. These are practice settings, not different ISSF clay targets or real shotgun patterns.

C1 with H1 is the more demanding combination; C2 with H2 is more forgiving. Choose settings deliberately, and compare records only when both CLAY and HIT settings match. Otherwise a higher score may just mean an easier configuration.

08What exactly does TRAP COACH analyse?

Depending on the recorded session, TRAP COACH can display target and device-motion traces, movement onset, the time from visible clay to the first shot, first/second-shot timing and virtual hit/miss events. These are measurements within the simulator and its sensors. COACH cannot directly measure your eye movements, finger pressure on the screen, real barrel path or live-range accuracy. Explore COACH.

09Can I practise one clay direction or shoot until my first miss?

In firmware builds that include the optional practice modes, you can repeat a left, straight or right clay flight to work on a particular movement. This is a focused drill, not a standard ISSF series.

UNTIL MISS is a separate challenge: the directions follow a randomized ISSF-style sequence and the run ends at the first missed target, or after a maximum of 25 targets. Try to beat your best uninterrupted streak while keeping the same CLAY and HIT settings.

Practice-mode availability and record saving depend on the installed firmware version. These training attempts should remain separate from standard 25-target series and their results.

10Is TRAPPUNK open source? Can I help improve it?

The website repository and the Coach RAG knowledge base are public. The main firmware source repository is still private; a public BIN download does not make that source open source or grant an MIT licence. You can already report bugs, suggest ideas and share experiments in GitHub Discussions.
